Inkbelly — a Violet pet feeder

I made a desktop pet. The pet is Violet (Deadlock), the food is
butterflies, and the score is kept in her body: a full gut tract
that fills as she eats. Stomach, bowel, collector, compactor, a
heart that pays out, and a bladder that fills whether you're
paying attention or not.

In auto mode she feeds herself and your job is management.
There's a shop, upgrades, bathroom breaks you buy for her, and a
little garden that turns mud back into butterflies. Neglect her
and her stress climbs — she strains, she leaks, and if it maxes
out she empties everything you failed to clear, right there,
and leaves for 30 minutes. She's busy with Billy, not you.

The part I actually care about: the rear window doesn't fake
anything. It's 151 animation variants and every one of them is
driven by her real bladder and colon levels. She only pees when
she's actually leaking or flushed, she only poops on a real
flush or the failure accident, and how much comes out is how
much was actually in there. Full looks full. That took the
longest to get right and it's the whole point.

There's a size picker when it starts — down to a tiny 368×310
window meant to sit in a corner while you play — and it remembers
what you picked.

Windows. Unzip, run Inkbelly-Auto.exe, no install.

Download: the four part files (Inkbelly-Auto-v45.zip.001 through .004) and JOIN-INKBELLY.bat, all in this folder. Put them in one folder together, double-click the bat, and it rebuilds the full zip — or just open the .001 file in 7-Zip. Then unzip and run Inkbelly-Auto.exe.
